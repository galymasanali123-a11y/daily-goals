# Medicine: first terms

A short page, not a textbook. The goal is to hear the words in a simple story.

## The body as a map

Think of the body as rooms connected by corridors.

- The **heart** is a pump. It sends **blood**.
- The **lungs** take in air. **Oxygen** goes into the blood.
- The **brain** is the control room.
- The **stomach** and **intestines** break down food.
- The **kidneys** filter the blood.
- **Bones** and **muscles** let you stand and move.
- The **skin** is the outer wall.

A **cell** is a tiny unit. Tissues are groups of cells. An **organ** is a working part (heart, liver). A **system** is a team of organs (the cardiovascular system).

## In the clinic

A **patient** comes with a **symptom** — something they feel (pain, fever, cough). A **sign** is something the clinician can see or measure (rash, high temperature).

Typical path:

1. **History** — questions: what, where, when, how bad?
2. **Examination** — look, listen, touch.
3. **Tests** if needed: blood test, **X-ray**, scan.
4. **Diagnosis** — the working name of the problem.
5. **Treatment** — medicine, rest, procedure, or watch and wait.

**Acute** means sudden. **Chronic** means long-lasting. **Benign** is not cancer. **Malignant** is cancer. Do not self-diagnose from a word list; these are reading words.
